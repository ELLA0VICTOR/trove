# Trove demo: one family, one learning journey

**About three minutes and forty seconds**, including the landing-page introduction. Edward is the parent. Eddie Junior is the learner. Read the narration calmly, like showing a friend how the app works.

The complete narration is in [DEMO-VOICEOVER.txt](DEMO-VOICEOVER.txt), ready to paste into the voiceover tool. The [full walkthrough](DEMO-WALKTHROUGH.md) gives each feature more time.

## Set the scene before recording

- Keep Edward signed in in one regular browser profile and Eddie Junior in another, with their corresponding Slush wallets. Keep both windows open.
- Use the existing family and activated portfolio. Keep the parent limit at **$50 per investment** and **$200 per day**, with practice active and enough available cash for the two example purchases.
- In Edward's **Home → Settings → Remember learning**, enable remembering. If already enabled, show that section briefly during the recording.
- Prepare Eddie's saved learning with the reflections below. Let the records reach **MAINNET CONFIRMED** before filming the memory section. Keep the first thesis available for the on-screen correction.
- Keep the Apple and Microsoft reasons ready to paste. Record the real actions in order, then trim loading and wallet-confirmation waits when editing.
- Capture the brief signup-screen clip in a separate signed-out window. Use Edward and Eddie's existing sessions for the rest of the recording.
- Record the correction and its follow-up answer before recording the later-session clip. For that clip, sign into Eddie's existing account again off camera.

## Text to paste during the recording

### Apple reason

> I know Apple products. I want to understand how Apple earns money and why its share price changes.

### Microsoft reason

> I want to compare Microsoft with Apple and understand what risks they share.

### First saved thesis

> I think owning Apple and Microsoft means I have spread my risk because they have different company names.

### First Ask Trove question

> Have I really spread my risk by owning Apple and Microsoft?

### Correction

> I now understand that different company names can share the same industry risk. I need to look at what the companies have in common.

### Follow-up question

> What changed in my understanding of spreading risk?

### Later-session question

> What do you remember about how I think about spreading risk?

Give the actual answers room on screen. Highlight the sentence that connects Eddie's earlier thesis to his updated understanding.
