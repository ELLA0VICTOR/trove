# Trove: the full family walkthrough

**About six to seven minutes**, with room to show each interaction. Edward is the parent and Eddie Junior is the learner. The spoken sections below form one continuous script. Screen directions are for recording.

Use [the recording setup and paste-ready text](DEMO-SCRIPT.md) to prepare the existing accounts. The [main demo voiceover](DEMO-VOICEOVER.txt) is the shorter cut of the same story.

## 1. Why Trove exists

**Screen:** Start on the landing page. Let the sapling and Trove name settle, then move toward Try now.

**Voiceover:**

“Children hear about companies, stocks, and investing all the time. But understanding what to buy, why to buy it, and what could go wrong takes practice.

Trove gives children a place to build that understanding with a parent or guardian. They make practice investments using real company prices, explain their choices, and learn from what happens.

Let's follow one family: Edward and Eddie Junior.”

## 2. How a family gets started

**Screen:** Briefly show the signup and join tabs in a signed-out window. Then cut to Edward's existing Home and open Settings. Point to Create learner invitation. Show Eddie's existing account in the other browser.

**Voiceover:**

“Edward creates a family account with his email and password, then verifies his email. From Settings, he creates an invitation for Eddie.

Eddie chooses Join a family, enters that code, and creates his own account. They have separate sign-ins and share one practice portfolio.

They can also change their usernames in Settings and reset a forgotten password through email. Our two accounts are already set up, so let's go inside.”

## 3. Remembering and wallet setup

**Screen:** Edward's Settings: show Remember learning and the enabled state. Show the parent wallet connection and active portfolio. Switch briefly to Eddie's wallet connection.

**Voiceover:**

“Edward turns on Remember learning. This lets Trove save Eddie's investment reasons, lesson results, and reflections, so future explanations can build on what he has already learned.

Edward connects his wallet and activates the family portfolio. Eddie connects his own wallet when he is ready to practise. Each person signs their own actions.”

## 4. Edward sets the boundaries

**Screen:** Edward's Parent page: show Per investment and Daily allowance. Open Edit limits, show 50 and 200, then close it. Point to Pause practice and Add practice funds.

**Voiceover:**

“Edward decides how much Eddie can use in one investment and across a day. Here, that is fifty dollars per investment and two hundred dollars per day.

These limits give Eddie room to make his own choices. Edward can change them, pause practice, or add more practice funds as they go.”

## 5. Eddie explores and buys Apple

**Screen:** Eddie's Home, then Explore. Briefly use the search or an industry filter. Open Apple and show the price, date, and chart. Choose Make a practice move, enter 25, and paste the Apple reason from the recording guide. Review, confirm, sign, and return to Home.

**Voiceover:**

“On Home, Eddie sees the family's practice balance, the companies he holds, and how his investments are doing.

In Explore, he can search for a company, compare industries, and look at prices and charts. Each price shows its closing date.

Eddie knows Apple products, so he wants to learn more about the company. He chooses twenty-five dollars and writes his reason.

He reviews the amount, price, and shares, then signs the practice trade. After confirmation, Apple appears in his holdings and his available cash updates.”

## 6. A bigger choice becomes a conversation

**Screen:** Eddie opens Microsoft, enters 75, pastes the Microsoft reason, and chooses Review my practice move, then Ask for guidance. Switch to Edward's Parent page. Approve this choice and sign. Switch back to Eddie's Home, choose Sign approved trade, and sign. Show Microsoft in holdings and the unchanged $50 limit.

**Voiceover:**

“Next, Eddie wants seventy-five dollars of Microsoft. That goes above his fifty-dollar limit, so he sends Edward a request with his reason.

Edward sees the company, amount, and what Eddie is thinking. He approves this choice with his wallet. Eddie then signs the trade from his own account.

The fifty-dollar limit stays in place for future choices. Eddie gets to try a bigger decision with Edward's guidance.”

Complete this scene's request and both signatures together; record other actions afterward.

## 7. Decisions keep the reason beside the result

**Screen:** Eddie's Decisions page. Open the Apple decision, show Original reason and the confirmed receipt, then Add a reflection. Save: “Liking a company's products gives me a reason to learn about it. I still need to understand the business and its risks.”

**Voiceover:**

“Decisions keeps a record of what Eddie chose and why. He can open a trade, read his original reason, and add what he understands differently now.

The practice actions are recorded on Sui Testnet. Sui enforces the family's trading rules, and Tatum checks the confirmed results before Trove updates the portfolio.”

## 8. Lessons and Replay make the ideas concrete

**Screen:** Learn → Lessons → Shared risk. Open Check understanding if it is the current lesson, choose the answer about different names sharing risk, then Check answer and Done. Open Decisions → Replay decisions. Adjust one allocation slider and move through the chart.

**Voiceover:**

“Learn gives Eddie short lessons about risk, time, costs, and how to judge a decision. A quick question helps him put each idea into his own thinking.

Replay lets him try different investment mixes over the same market period. He can change the mix and see how the results compare. That gives the lesson something he can explore for himself.”

If Shared risk is already completed, open its review and move directly to Replay.

## 9. Ask Trove connects the explanation to Eddie

**Screen:** Learn → Saved thoughts. Show the original Apple-and-Microsoft thesis and MAINNET CONFIRMED. Close it, choose Ask Trove, and send the first question from the recording guide. Show the answer and its saved-thought references.

**Voiceover:**

“Here is something Eddie saved earlier: he thinks owning Apple and Microsoft means he has spread his risk because they have different names.

These thoughts are saved with Walrus Memory on Mainnet. Trove can bring them back when Eddie asks for help.

He asks, ‘Have I really spread my risk?’ The guide uses his own saved thinking to explain what the two companies might have in common. Eddie can open the saved thoughts behind the answer.”

## 10. A correction changes the next explanation

**Screen:** Open Saved thoughts. Find the original thesis, choose Correct this thought, paste the correction, and save. Cut forward to the replacement's confirmation. Ask the follow-up question and show the response. For the next shot, sign into Eddie's account again off camera and ask the later-session question.

**Voiceover:**

“Eddie has learned something new. Different company names can still share the same risks. He corrects his earlier thought and saves what he understands now.

When he asks what changed, Trove uses that updated understanding. When he returns in another session, the learning history is still there.

His earlier choice, the question it raised, and the lesson he took from it all stay connected.”

## 11. Edward can adjust the practice as Eddie grows

**Screen:** Edward's Parent page. Add practice funds → enter 100 → Add funds → sign. Show the updated cash. Pause practice, sign, show Practice paused; then resume and sign. Switch to Eddie's Home. In Apple, briefly show the Practice sell tab and return to Home.

**Voiceover:**

“Edward can add more practice funds while keeping Eddie's holdings and learning history. He can also pause practice and resume it when they are ready.

Eddie can practise selling part of a holding too, and see how that changes his cash and investments.

As new market prices arrive, the value of what he holds can rise or fall. He can come back, look at the result, and think about the reason behind his choice.”

## 12. Close on the purpose

**Screen:** Eddie's portfolio, a saved thought, then the Trove name and trove-x.vercel.app.

**Voiceover:**

“Edward sets the boundaries. Eddie makes choices, asks questions, and builds his understanding over time.

Trove gives families a way to learn about investing together, while children build the habits they can carry into adulthood.

One choice. One conversation. A little more understanding.”
